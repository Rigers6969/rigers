# Learn Premiere and After Effects, Channel by Channel

**Short answer:** Learn Premiere first. Learn After Effects second. For Premiere, start with **Gavin Herman**: watch his 10-minute beginner video first (2M+ views), then his 76-minute full course. YouTube's own data confirms that both videos are his. For the new 2026 features, follow **Kyler Holland**. He writes up every new version, including **single-word captions (version 26.3, June 2026)**. For After Effects, start with **Adobe's free 3–4 hour workshop with Kyle Hamrick**, then **Ben Marriott**. For your channels, the main teachers are: **Flat Pack FX** for Paper Trail (graphs, newspapers, timelines), **Boone Loves Video** and Flat Pack FX for History (maps), **Ben Marriott** and **SonduckFilm** for The Forgotten Lab (clean shapes and text), and **Kyler Holland** for Hot Mic Moments (captions). **No channel teaches the Chat Lost It layout**, but a few short split-screen videos and one simple layout rule cover it. In 2026 you can start all five channels in Premiere alone. After Effects adds the stronger graphics later. Your PC can run both apps, but only at the minimum. Adobe's 2026 apps need **Windows 11 24H2**, After Effects needs **16 GB RAM**, and your 4 GB graphics card is exactly at the floor. Be careful with numbers. Subscriber counts are snapshots from tracker sites, and YouTube pages could not be opened during this research. Many tutorial videos have an unknown uploader, and they are marked.

## How to read this report

- **[CONFIRMED]** means YouTube's own data (YouTube Data API, 18 Oct 2026) confirmed the channel, the view count and the upload date of that video. View counts are from that day and are higher now.
- **"Uploader not confirmed"** means the video is real (it came up in search results), but we do not know which channel posted it. Nobody watched it. Watch the first minute before you trust it.
- **[SELLS]** means the teacher sells a course, plugin, preset, template or tool. The free videos are enough to learn. You do not need to buy anything.
- **Subscriber numbers** come from tracker sites (Social Blade, vidIQ, Playboard and others) on the date shown. They are not live numbers. "Not verified" means we found no reliable number.
- **New name:** Adobe renamed "Premiere Pro" to **"Adobe Premiere"** with version 26.0 on 20 Jan 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe_Premiere_Pro)). On YouTube, search for both names. Most tutorials still say "Premiere Pro".
- **Tips for basic English:** play videos at 0.75x speed, turn on subtitles (use auto-translate if you need it), and use the chapters. Written guides (Kyler Holland, Motion Array, Adobe Help) are easy to put into a translator.

### Premiere's built-in 2026 features you will use

- **Single-word captions (26.3, June 2026).** Premiere now makes captions that show one word at a time, built from the transcript. When you create captions, choose "Single Word" (Create Captions → Captioning Preferences → Layout → Single Word) ([Adobe Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-text-images/insert-captions/create-single-word-captions.html); [Black Hat Editors](https://blackhateditors.com/blog/en/auto-subtitles-premiere-pro/)). **Limit:** as of 26.5 the words do not animate by themselves. There is no pop, no karaoke fill and no color per word ([Black Hat Editors](https://blackhateditors.com/blog/en/best-caption-plugins-premiere-pro/)). Fits: Hot Mic Moments, Chat Lost It, all Shorts.
  - Learn it: Kyler Holland's written tutorial, with 20 free text styles ([kylerholland.com](https://www.kylerholland.com/blog/single-word-captions-premiere-pro-tutorial)).
  - Video: "Premiere Pro FINALLY Added Single Word Captions" (about July 2026) — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tsZ1a3JmMHg. Uploader not confirmed, but it matches Kyler Holland's tutorial.
- **Auto Reframe.** It turns a wide video into vertical 9:16 and follows the action ([Adobe Help](https://learn.adobe.com/premiere-pro/using/auto-reframe.html)). Steps: right-click the sequence → Auto Reframe Sequence → Vertical 9:16 → choose a motion-tracking speed → Create ([Hollyland](https://store.hollyland.com/blogs/creator-hub/use-auto-reframe-in-premiere-pro)). **Watch out:** from a 1920×1080 sequence it can give a frame only 607 px wide. Fix it by using a 1080×1920 sequence ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/t5/premiere-pro-discussions/auto-reframe-frame-size-wrong/m-p/14620848)). Fits: Hot Mic Moments, Shorts made from your long videos.
  - Video: "How To Resize Video For YouTube Shorts & TikTok In Premiere (Convert Horizontal to Vertical Video)" — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nEUdlIizSeA (uploader not confirmed)
- **Text-based editing.** You cut the video by deleting words in the transcript. Fits: Hot Mic Moments (find the best line fast) and cleaning up voiceovers.
  - Video: "How to Transcribe and Edit Video by Text in Premiere Pro" — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2XReg3P0n8o (uploader not confirmed)
  - New in 26.5 (September 2026): **Paper Edit** turns a transcript into a rough cut ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/announcements-727/what-s-new-in-adobe-premiere-26-5-september-2026-1641187)).
- **Enhance Speech.** It cleans up a voice recording. Select the clip → Essential Sound → Enhance → use the Mix Amount slider ([Adobe Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-audio-effects/adjust-volume-and-levels/enhance-speech.html)). Fits: your voiceovers and podcast clips.
- **Auto Ducking.** It lowers the music under the voice automatically (in Essential Sound). Fits: Paper Trail, History, The Forgotten Lab.
  - Video: "How to balance voice over and background music with Auto Ducking in Premiere Pro" — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m7qqxt24u2Y (uploader not confirmed)
- **Remix.** It makes a music track shorter or longer to fit your edit: Clip → Remix → Enable Remix ([ifilmthings](https://ifilmthings.com/remix-tool-in-premiere-pro/)). It works better for shortening than for lengthening ([Larry Jordan](https://larryjordan.com/articles/the-new-remix-tool-in-adobe-premiere-pro-beta)).
- **Translate Captions** became a standard feature in 26.0 ([Kyler Holland](https://www.kylerholland.com/blog/premiere-pro-january-2026-whats-new)).
- **Generative Extend** adds up to 2 seconds of video or 10 seconds of audio (not speech or music). It needs an internet connection ([Adobe Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/ca/premiere/desktop/edit-projects/edit-with-generative-ai/add-frames-using-generative-extend.html)).

**One overview video of the new features:** Enam Alamin, "15 Premiere Pro Features Every Editor Should Know (2026)" — https://youtu.be/VmWWQEyZmrk — 14:26, 107,597 views, uploaded 15 Jul 2026 **[CONFIRMED]**. His channel link and subscriber count are not in our notes.

## 2. After Effects basics: Adobe's free workshop, then Ben Marriott

**What After Effects is for:** graphics. Maps, charts, money counters, newspapers and animated shapes. You still edit the video in Premiere.

**How the two apps work together:**
- **Dynamic Link:** in Premiere, select clips, right-click, choose "Replace With After Effects Composition", and save the After Effects project. When you change it in After Effects, Premiere updates without rendering ([Adobe Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/use-premiere-with-other-apps/working-with-other-adobe-applications/replace-clips-with-a-dynamically-linked-after-effects-composition.html)). Both apps must be on the same version. For heavy graphics, use Clip > Render and Replace for smoother playback ([Adobe Help](https://helpx.adobe.com/after-effects/using/dynamic-link-effects.html)).
  - Video: "Adobe Premiere Pro and After Effects Dynamic Link Tutorial – Workflow Tips for Editors" — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kNN_wk40kBY (uploader not confirmed)
- **MOGRT (Motion Graphics Template):** a graphic you build once in After Effects and reuse in Premiere's Essential Graphics panel. Our tip: build your money counter, lower titles and caption style once, then reuse them in every video.

## 5. Will it run on my PC? Yes, but only at the minimum

**Short answer:** Premiere will work for 1080p videos. After Effects will work for simple 2D graphics, but it will be slow with heavy effects, 3D or 4K.

| Part | Premiere 26 needs | After Effects 26 needs | Your PC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Windows | **Windows 11 24H2** | **Windows 11 24H2** | Check yours. Version 26.3 no longer supports Windows 10 ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-729/win10-extended-support-until-2027-why-are-you-forcing-us-to-switch-to-win11-with-v-26-3-1629680)) |
| Processor | Intel 6th gen or newer, with AVX2 | Same (11th gen recommended) | i7-8700 is 8th gen: OK |
| Graphics card | 4 GB VRAM minimum | 4 GB minimum; Adobe also notes 8 GB | GTX 1630 4 GB: exactly at the minimum |
| RAM | 8 GB minimum, 16 GB for HD | **16 GB minimum**, 32 GB for 4K | Check yours. 16 GB is the floor for After Effects |

Sources: [Adobe Premiere requirements](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/get-started/technical-requirements/adobe-premiere-pro-technical-requirements.html), [Adobe Premiere Windows version](https://helpx.adobe.com/sg/premiere-pro/system-requirements.html), [Adobe After Effects requirements](https://helpx.adobe.com/fi/after-effects/desktop/get-started/technical-requirements/system-requirements.html).

**About your graphics card:** the GTX 1630 has 4 GB on a narrow 64-bit bus, about half the bandwidth of a GTX 1650. In one games test it was slower than the old GTX 1050 Ti (37 vs 43 fps) ([Noticias3D](https://www.noticias3d.com/noticia/89219/hardware-unboxed-pone-prueba-geforce-gtx-1630-considera-mala.html)). Good news: it can export H.264 and HEVC with its hardware encoder ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/video-encode-decode-support-matrix?ncid=em-prod-816193)). No After Effects test on this card was found.

**Tips to make it faster:**
- Edit at 1080p, not 4K. Adobe's guidance is 4 GB of graphics memory for HD ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/dk/after-effects/desktop/get-started/technical-requirements/2024.html)).
- In After Effects, lower the preview resolution (Half, Quarter or Auto). Put the disk cache on a fast SSD that is not your footage drive. Pre-render nested comps. Animate first, and add heavy effects (blur, glow) last ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/uk/after-effects/using/improve-performance.html)).
- Do not run Premiere and After Effects together for heavy work. Premiere takes RAM away from After Effects previews ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/uk/after-effects/using/memory-storage1.html)).
- For heavy linked graphics, use Render and Replace in Premiere (section 2).
- Shift+Numpad 0 previews every other frame. Convert phone H.264 footage to an editing codec such as ProRes ([Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-529/what-are-the-best-settings-to-optimize-performance-and-ram-preview-60637)).
- Use NVIDIA **Studio** drivers ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/get-started/technical-requirements/gpu-and-gpu-driver-requirements.html)).
- The best upgrades are more RAM (32 GB) and a fast NVMe SSD for the cache (our view, based on Adobe's tips).

**Cost:** Premiere is about $22.99/month and After Effects about $22.99/month on yearly plans, so about $552 a year for both ([Miracamp](https://www.miracamp.com/learn/premiere-pro/price); [Photutorial](https://photutorial.com/after-effects-pricing)).

## 6. Start here: a simple learning order

1. **Check your PC.** Windows 11 24H2? How much RAM? NVIDIA Studio driver? Install Premiere and After Effects (same version).
2. **Learn Premiere basics.** Gavin Herman's 10-minute video, then his full course, one chapter a day.
3. **Learn the 2026 features.** Read Kyler Holland's 26.3 post and watch Enam Alamin's "15 features" video. Practice single-word captions and Auto Reframe.
4. **Make your first Shorts.** Hot Mic Moments: captions, punch-in zoom, export (Daniel Grindrod's video). Chat Lost It: build and save the 40/60 facecam template.
5. **Learn hooks.** Kallaway's "6 hooks" video and one Jenny Hoyos interview. Use them on every Short.
6. **Make your first documentary in Premiere only.** Motion Array's Johnny Harris guide, Ken Burns moves, the newspaper highlight, the map line, a Premiere counter, Auto Ducking and Envato's sound design video.
7. **Learn scripts and retention.** George Blackman and Film Booth. Follow vidIQ's rule: 3 test videos, change one thing at a time.
8. **Learn After Effects basics.** Kyle Hamrick's free workshop (or Ben Marriott's 10-minute video, then his 60-minute video). Learn Dynamic Link.
9. **Learn After Effects for each channel.**
   - Paper Trail: the money counter, then Flat Pack FX's newspaper and graph videos.
   - History: a no-plugin map tutorial, then Flat Pack FX's timeline. Buy GEOlayers and follow Boone Loves Video only when the channel is growing.
   - The Forgotten Lab: Ben Marriott's shape animation, SonduckFilm's Kurzgesagt-style series, and Kyle Hamrick's text animators.
10. **Save your best graphics as templates (MOGRTs)** and reuse them in every video.

## Conclusion

In 2026, Premiere can carry all five channels on its own at the start. Single-word captions, Auto Reframe, Premiere-only counters, newspaper highlights and Ken Burns moves cover the basics. After Effects is an upgrade, not a day-one requirement. This matters on your PC: you can delay the heaviest app until a channel shows it can grow, and spend that time on hooks and scripts, which decide retention more than effects do.

The best teachers for your niches are small specialists, not the biggest channels. Flat Pack FX (about 173K), Boone Loves Video and Kyler Holland (about 300K) teach exactly what Paper Trail, History and Hot Mic Moments need. Cinecom (2.7M) and Justin Odisho (1.14M) are far bigger, but their growth is flat and they teach general effects. So choose teachers by topic, not by subscriber count. Also treat most technique videos as single lessons, not channels to follow, because their uploaders are still unknown.
