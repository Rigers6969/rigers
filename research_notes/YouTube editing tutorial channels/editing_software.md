## Key question 1: Which YouTube channels best teach each program?

### Takeaway
- **DaVinci Resolve:** Casey Faris is the strongest pick. He has a 2026 full beginner course of about 5 hours and about 618K–634K subscribers in 2026. Kevin Stratvert (4.48M subscribers, Oct 2026) and MrAlexTech are good backups. Jason Yadlovski is a smaller certified trainer.
- **Premiere Pro:** Start with Adobe's own channel (Valentina Vee's "Premiere Pro For Beginners | 2025 Edition"). Then use Justin Odisho (about 1.13M) and Premiere Gal (about 559K–575K) for short technique videos.
- **After Effects motion-graphics basics:** Ben Marriott (about 924K, Oct 2026), Jake In Motion (about 256K, Apr 2026) and SonduckFilm (about 1.16M, Jun 2026).
- **CapCut:** I found no reliable ranking. Primal Video (Justin Brown, about 1.88M) is the best-documented teacher, with 2025–2026 desktop and mobile guides. Kevin Stratvert's channel has a beginner CapCut tutorial from 2024 (older), hosted by Casey Faris.

## Key question 2: Does each program run well on a GTX 1630 / i7-8700 PC, and what does each cost in 2026?

### Takeaway
- **All four programs will install.** The GTX 1630 has exactly the 4 GB of VRAM that Premiere 26.x, After Effects 26.x and Resolve 21 now list as the minimum. It sits at the floor, not comfortably above it.
- **Windows version:** Adobe's 2026 apps (26.x) require **Windows 11 24H2**. Resolve 21 still lists Windows 10.
- **System RAM is the other limit.** Resolve and AE want 16 GB at minimum, and Fusion in Resolve wants 32 GB. The PC's RAM is not stated in the brief.
- **Cost:**

  | Program | 2026 price (USD) |
  |---|---|
  | DaVinci Resolve | Free; Studio is a one-time $295 |
  | Premiere Pro | About $22.99/month on an annual plan |
  | After Effects | About $22.99/month on an annual plan |
  | CapCut | Free; Pro about $19.99/month or $179.99/year (US) |

### Cited Findings

#### The GPU (GTX 1630)
- **Hardware encoding:** NVIDIA's support matrix lists the GTX 1630 with NVENC (1 encoder).
  - H.264 and HEVC 4K 4:2:0 encoding are supported.
  - HEVC 10-bit encoding, HEVC B-frames and 4:2:2 are marked unsupported.
  - The matrix gives a 12-session limit, while a January 2024 report said 3 sessions — [NVIDIA Video Encode/Decode Support Matrix](https://developer.nvidia.com/video-encode-decode-support-matrix?ncid=em-prod-816193); [Profesional Review, 2024](https://www.profesionalreview.com/2024/01/31/nvidia-nvenc-twitch/)
- **No hardware AV1 decode;** other media features are consistent with the Turing family — [TechPowerUp forum review post](https://www.techpowerup.com/forums/goto/post?id=4784021)

#### Premiere Pro 26.x (2026)
- **Windows:** Windows 11 v24H2 is the minimum — [Adobe Premiere technical requirements](https://helpx.adobe.com/sg/premiere-pro/system-requirements.html)
  - Premiere Pro 26 ended Windows 10 support — [BornCity](https://borncity.com/news/premiere-pro-26-adobe-beendet-windows-10-support-komplett/)
  - In June 2026, users reported that 26.3 no longer supports Windows 10 — [Adobe Community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-729/win10-extended-support-until-2027-why-are-you-forcing-us-to-switch-to-win11-with-v-26-3-1629680)

- **CPU:** Intel 6th Gen or newer with AVX2 — [Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/fi/premiere-pro/system-requirements.html)
- **GPU:** the 26.x page lists a **4 GB VRAM minimum**, with NVIDIA cards of the Maxwell generation or newer.
