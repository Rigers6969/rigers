# YouTube channels that teach retention, storytelling and packaging (with a faceless-channel lens)

Research date: 2026-10-26. Method notes, so the writer knows how much to trust each number:
- youtube.com, socialblade.com and reddit.com could not be opened directly from this research environment. YouTube refused the connection, Social Blade did not resolve, and Reddit is blocked for the search tool. So subscriber counts and video facts come from **search-engine snapshots** of Social Blade, SpeakRJ, HypeAuditor and YouTube pages. Each number below gives its source and the snapshot date when the snapshot showed one. Counts marked "undated" come from cached YouTube page metadata, so they could be weeks or months old.
- Every video title and URL below appeared in search results, so the URLs are real. When I could not confirm **which channel uploaded** a video (it may be an interview on someone else's channel), it is marked "uploader not confirmed".
- No Reddit threads were reachable (r/NewTubers, r/PartneredYoutube and r/youtubers were all blocked), so community reputation comes from interviews, reviews and articles, not from Reddit.

## Q1. Which channels are most respected for teaching YouTube retention, storytelling and packaging? (per-channel profiles)

### Takeaway
The strongest teachers of the **"why"** behind editing and packaging are Paddy Galloway, Film Booth (Ed Lawrence), George Blackman for scripting, Aprilynne Alter, Jake Thomas (Creator Hooks) for titles, and Colin and Samir for creator-economy context. For Shorts, Kallaway (hooks) and Jenny Hoyos (Shorts retention) stand out. Two big caveats: **Paddy Galloway's own channel is dormant** (33 videos), so he is learned mostly through interviews, and **Jake Thomas has no teaching channel of his own that I could find**, so his lessons come through interviews and his newsletter. Think Media is huge but generalist and built around on-camera creators, so it fits a faceless documentary channel poorly.

## Q2. Which channels teach faceless (documentary or automation-style) YouTube well, and which are course-sellers or get-rich-quick?

### Takeaway
I found **no faceless-specific teacher that is clearly free of selling**. The most credible faceless-focused source is **vidIQ**. It is a tool company and its videos promote vidIQ, but its faceless advice is practical and data-based: test, and read impressions, CTR and retention. Most "faceless" tutorial results are AI-automation, "$X/day" content, which is a get-rich-quick red flag. YouTube's crackdown on mass-produced AI content makes that approach risky. The best path is to apply the general retention and story teachers above to the faceless format.

### Cited Findings
- **vidIQ**: https://www.youtube.com/vidiq/videos. ### Inferences
- For a business-fraud, history or science documentary channel, the **AI-automation faceless teachers are the wrong model**. They push volume, which is exactly what the "mass-produced" policy targets. Script and retention teachers (Blackman, Film Booth, Paddy, Aprilynne) plus vidIQ's testing discipline are the safer combination.
- Almost every teacher here sells something: Film Booth (a 90-day system), Blackman (a playbook), Aprilynne (a cohort), vidIQ (software), Matt Par and Caleb Boxx (courses). The honest line to draw is between people with **verifiable client or channel results who teach principles** (Paddy, Blackman, Aprilynne, Ed Lawrence) and those who **sell income claims** ("$700/day").

### Inferences
- A clip channel's main editing decisions are the first 1–2 seconds (the hook) and the end (cut at the peak, let it loop). These match what Kallaway and Jenny Hoyos teach, though Hoyos's "25% drop in last second" figure is unverified.
- For fraud, history and science documentaries, script structure (open loops, stakes, re-hooks) drives retention more than editing tricks. That makes Blackman and Film Booth the first two to study.
