# YouTube channels that teach faceless documentary / explainer editing (maps, documents, charts, parallax, motion graphics)

> Research date: 26 Oct 2026. **How this was checked:** In this research environment, YouTube, Social Blade and Reddit pages could not be opened directly (WebFetch DNS failure, proxy 403). Everything below comes from web-search results: page titles, URLs, snippets and structured data from YouTube, Social Blade, vidIQ, Playboard, PremiumBeat, aescripts, Teachable and Gumroad. As a result:
> - Every URL listed was returned by a search engine as a real page. None was invented.
> - Subscriber counts list their source and date. Where a number came only from undated YouTube page metadata ("interactionCount"), it is marked as undated.
> - For many single tutorial videos, the search results gave the title and URL but **not the uploading channel**. These are marked "channel not confirmed".
> - Upload ages shown as "~N days old" come from search metadata and are approximate.

## Q1. Which YouTube channels teach documentary / explainer editing techniques (animated maps, document/newspaper reveals, chart animations, parallax photos, kinetic text, sound design)?

### Takeaway
The best verified teaching channels for this niche are **map specialists** (Boone Loves Video, Flat Pack FX), the **documentary/motion-graphics generalist SonduckFilm**, and **motion-design fundamentals channels** (Ben Marriott, Dope Motions, Jake In Motion, School of Motion). Almost all of this teaching uses **Adobe After Effects**, and the map channels rely heavily on the paid **GEOlayers 3** plugin. Newspaper reveals, Vox-style parallax and chart animation are mostly taught in single videos and playlists by smaller creators, not by one dominant channel. No YouTube channel focused on documentary sound design turned up.

### Cited Findings
**Map animation (most relevant to History, also useful for Paper Trail)**

It also offers a Patreon with project files and presets. — [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/channel/UCXyGw2OkrAzLhq1r7hyDZkA); [aescripts tag page](https://aescripts.com/learn/tag/boone-loves-video)

- Boone sells a paid "GEOlayers 3 WAR!" course for battle maps. Its lessons cover collecting maps, a battle-map breakdown, georeferencing, digitizing and label animation. — [Teachable](https://boonelovesvideo.teachable.com/p/geolayers-3-war)
- **Flat Pack FX** (run by "Ross") teaches video effects, animation and map animation in After Effects. It sells a GEOlayers 3 Maps Mini Course. — [YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/@FlatPackFX); [videos tab](https://www.youtube.com/c/FlatPackFX/videos)

### Inferences
- Because almost every strong tutorial is After Effects-based, the three channels will probably need After Effects even if the main edit is in Premiere or DaVinci. Premiere-only Vox/newspaper tutorials exist and can serve as a lighter alternative.
- GEOlayers is effectively the standard tool for map-heavy channels (Johnny Harris, Boone, Flat Pack FX). Budgeting for it is likely worthwhile for the History channel. SonduckFilm's no-plugin map tutorials are the free fallback.

### Gaps
- No Reddit threads (r/VideoEditing, r/AfterEffects, r/NewTubers) could be retrieved, so community recommendations are missing.
- No dedicated documentary sound-design teaching channel was found.
- No OverSimplified-style tutorial was found.
- For a DaVinci Resolve user, Casey Faris has a "Travel Map Animation in DaVinci Resolve! - Fusion Beginner Tutorial" (dated 22 Feb 2024, about 47,287 views according to a third-party summary). Neither the video's YouTube URL nor the channel URL was found, so it is not listed as a recommendation. — [lilys.ai summary](https://lilys.ai/en/notes/map-animation-20260119/travel-map-animation-davinci-resolve-fusion)

## Q3. Which channel fits Paper Trail, History, or The Forgotten Lab best, and why?

### Takeaway
**History** fits Boone Loves Video best, then Flat Pack FX: maps are the core visual, and both teach GEOlayers maps, including battle and historical maps. **Paper Trail** fits SonduckFilm best for documentary motion graphics, plus standalone chart and newspaper tutorials and MagnatesMedia-style breakdowns. **The Forgotten Lab** fits Ben Marriott best, then Dope Motions and School of Motion, for clean motion design and kinetic type in a Kurzgesagt-like direction.

### Gaps
- No tutorial specifically teaching an animated "money counter" or rolling number was found in this research.
